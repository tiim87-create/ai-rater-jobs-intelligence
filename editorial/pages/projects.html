Historical recruitment titles

Rater, US Rater (United States), and Internet Assessor are archived job-title aliases associated with Search Quality Rating. These are historical labels, not current vacancies. See the project profile for context and the Intelligence Matrix for tracked openings.

Best for: search intent, web research and nuanced judgment.

Search Quality Rating is one of the foundational human-evaluation programs behind Google Search. Raters interpret queries and evaluate search experiences using concepts such as Needs Met, Page Quality and Side-by-Side comparison. Human ratings are aggregated quality signals used to evaluate systems and proposed changes; an individual rating does not directly set a page’s ranking.

Project model: Query → Search Result / Experience → Human Judgment → Aggregated Quality Signal.

Typical path: Application → Guidelines / Qualification → General Pool → Production → Possible Specialized Eligibility.

How much can you earn? Our current working benchmark for U.S. Search Quality work is approximately $14.50/hour; India is approximately $4/hour. These are research benchmarks and must be tied to dated official recruitment evidence before public launch. Actual monthly earnings can be substantially lower when task availability is weak.

Known TELUS recruitment labels: Internet Safety Evaluator (explicit title), Content Evaluator (first-hand mapping), and Evaluator — US Ref. 129038 (first-hand mapping; public title does not establish the workflow). View the canonical-project vacancy filter.

Best for: evaluators with strong contextual judgment, good web-research habits and comfort reviewing potentially upsetting or offensive video material.

Internet Safety is better understood as video search quality + contextual safety evaluation than simple content moderation. Current TELUS Digital recruitment describes work reviewing video search results for relevance, accuracy and appropriateness, identifying misleading or potentially harmful material, and deciding how sensitive content should be treated in context.

Evaluation model: Video / Search Result → Primary Purpose & Creator Intent → Sensitive Topic → Metadata → Creator / Channel Reputation → Source Reliability → Potential Harm or Deception → Contextual Judgment.

Why context matters

The framework does not treat the mere presence of a sensitive topic as sufficient evidence of low quality or harm. Educational, documentary, news, fictional and other legitimate contexts can contain violence, hate speech, misinformation claims or other sensitive material while serving a beneficial purpose. The evaluator must distinguish that context from content that promotes, glorifies, deceives or creates meaningful risk.

What evaluators actually investigate

Typical judgment areas include important or sensitive topics, creator intent, metadata, channel and creator reputation, experience/expertise/authoritativeness/trust, misinformation, scams and deceptive practices, self-harm, violent or graphic material, dangerous content and hate speech. Research becomes especially important when claims concern medical, scientific, election or other high-impact topics.

Qualification

First-hand qualification structure: Internet Safety uses a multi-stage assessment designed to test both language ability and the application of project guidelines to real video content.

Language Proficiency Test → Critical Thinking Test → Guidelines-Based Theory Test → Practical YouTube Evaluation → Production.

The final practical stage requires the candidate to evaluate 20 YouTube videos. The passing score is 80%, with 2 attempts available. This makes the qualification more than a theory exam: candidates must demonstrate that they can apply the evaluation framework consistently to actual video content.

What the work feels like

First-hand perspective: for an experienced rater, Internet Safety can become more intuitive than some Search Quality or Factuality workflows once the evaluation framework is understood. Video provides richer contextual information than a bare query or isolated claim. The difficult cases are usually not obvious harmful content, but ambiguous material where intent, reputation, audience and context point in different directions.

Ads Quality Rating

PROJECT PROFILE

Vendors: TELUS Digital / legacy Lionbridge and other vendors by market

Geography: Multiple country/language markets

Pay rates: Vendor- and market-specific; consult official vacancy

Read the detailed project analysis below ↓

Best for: commercial intent, advertising relevance and evaluators who prefer structured workflows.

Ads Quality is a long-running human evaluation program, not one single task. At its core, the evaluator determines whether an advertising system is making a useful decision for the person behind a query or user context.

Historical model: Query → User Intent → Ad Creative → Landing Page → Human Judgment.

Broader modern model: User / Query → Advertiser or Targeting → Ad → Landing Experience → Human Judgment → Advertising Quality Signal.

EWOQ: the production environment

The Google-hosted EWOQ Rating Portal has historically been central to Ads evaluation. First-hand screenshots show both qualification and production states. The portal includes Things I Can Work On, task acquisition, capability-based access and Daily / Weekly / Monthly submission history.

This supports a more precise platform model:

Rater Account → Enabled Capabilities → Eligible Workflows → Available Tasks → Rating → Submission → Production History.

When no eligible work is available, EWOQ can display No tasks available and suggest changing capabilities. This means EWOQ should not be understood as one undifferentiated queue.

Multiple Ads workflows

Across different periods, documented or rater-reported workflows have included Search Ads Evaluation, Search Experience to Ad Usefulness, User to Keyword Evaluation, User Interest Evaluation, Query & Mobile App Match, Close Match, Ad Targeting Evaluation, Page Evaluation and mobile / QR-related tasks.

The important distinction is that Ads Quality Rating = project ecosystem; EWOQ = evaluation environment; workflow = a particular evaluation problem.

Qualification and blind quality control

First-hand EWOQ visual evidence shows a qualification state in which completed qualification tasks are followed by employer notification of next steps. Historical operational experience from 2018–2021 also shows that passing qualification was only the first quality gate.

During that period, the client periodically inserted blind quality-control tasks into production. A first portion typically appeared near the beginning of the month, with another portion sometimes appearing around mid-month. The monthly quality cycle was roughly 90–100 tasks in practice, although distribution varied.

Important historical quality families included Search Ads Evaluation (~55), User to Keyword Evaluation (~28) and User Interest Evaluation (~28). These figures are historical operational observations, not permanent quotas, and the category totals should not be interpreted as proof that every rater received all nominal tasks every month.

Quality lifecycle: Entrance Qualification → Production → Normal EWOQ Work + Blind Quality Tasks → Continuous Quality Measurement → Continued Eligibility / Review.

First-hand scale: 2018–2021

FIRST-HAND EXPERIENCE. Between 2018 and 2021, Ads Rating was the flagship program in the author’s evaluation operation, involving more than 200 raters. That scale provided visibility into recruitment, qualification, production stability, task availability and continuous quality control beyond the perspective of an individual evaluator.

EWOQ platform visual

The screenshot makes the platform logic visible: Rater Account → Enabled Capabilities → Eligible Workflows → Available Tasks → Rating → Submission → Production History. The Things I Can Work On control is especially important evidence that EWOQ does not operate as one undifferentiated task queue.

How Much Can You Earn?

Ads compensation varies substantially by locale and is often task-based with an approximate hourly equivalent. Published rates do not guarantee task volume.

U.S. note: $14.50/hour is retained as a historical Ads benchmark, not presented as a current 2026 U.S. offer.
